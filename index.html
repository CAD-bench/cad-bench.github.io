Benchmark

A CAD model may appear plausible, but it could have a misaligned hole, incompatible threads, or gears that collide rather than mesh. CAD-bench verifies that language-model agents produce designs that fulfill the task — not just generate syntactically valid or reference-like output. Our public benchmark consists of 17 CAD tasks.

These range from simple shapes to complex assemblies of holes, slots, threaded components and working gearboxes. In addition to verifying geometrical attributes like sizes, positions, topologies and mating features, our gearbox tasks also rely on a rigid-body physics simulation (using Blender) to check mechanical functionality. This allows us to provide a metric that distinguishes between a system that simply generates an artifact and a system that produces a design that works as intended.

Key results

The leaderboard displays the overall performance of complete runs of standalone models and agents, specifying the reasoning mode and internet access. The overall score combines the mean scores of four difficulty tiers with weights of 1, 2, 3 and 4. The final tier is labelled Extreme in the results and contains the functional tasks. The table includes cost, time and token counts in addition to the score.

Caveats

These automated tests should not be viewed as indicators that a design is manufacturable. The geometrical constraints and contact simulation techniques they rely upon have limitations; the paper includes a validation study and several examples of scoring failures. We provide the source code for our automated tests and the task specifications publicly.
