Beyond a plausible shape

A CAD model can look convincing and still be mechanically wrong: the hole might be in the wrong spot, the threads might not mesh, or the gears might collide instead of transmitting motion. CAD-bench measures whether the generated design solves the specified task, beyond whether its code runs or its shape resembles a reference.

Public CAD-bench consists of 17 tasks of varying complexity, from basic solids to models with holes and slots, to threaded joints and functional gear assemblies. The graders check dimensions, position, topology and mating geometry. Gearbox tasks also use Blender rigid-body simulation to test motion. The evaluation distinguishes between whether the submitted artifact builds and whether it meets the mechanical requirements.

Main results

The leaderboard compares complete standalone-model and agent runs, with their reasoning settings and internet access. Overall scores combine the mean scores of four difficulty tiers, weighted 1, 2, 3 and 4 respectively. The last tier is labelled Insane in the results and contains the functional tasks. Cost, time and token usage appear alongside scores.

Explore the tasks

Open full screen ↗

Try out all 17 public tasks in the interactive viewer. You can rotate each reference model, view its annotated dimensions and features, and compare it with an example of a common built failure. You can also inspect the evaluation panel, which describes the grader and how each validation rule is weighted in the score calculation.

Scope and limitations

The tests implemented by the grader are diagnostic tests. They don't guarantee that a CAD model is suitable for manufacturing. The geometric checks and contact simulation are inherently limited. The paper describes the process to validate the scorers and shows examples of their failures. The code and task descriptions are public.
