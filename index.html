CAD-bench
Benchmarking Language Models
on Functional CAD Generation

Dhruv Saini
Paper ↗ Code & tasks ↗ Leaderboard ↓ Model viewer ↓

Abstract

Language-model agents are increasingly able to operate computer-aided design (CAD) toolchains, but current evaluations often measure only executable code, rendered appearance, or coarse geometric similarity. These proxies can miss the properties that matter for reliable engineering artifacts: exact dimensions, mating interfaces, standards-like details, and functional behavior of assemblies. We introduce CAD-bench, an execution-based benchmark for evaluating language-model CAD agents.

CAD-bench contains 17 tasks across four difficulty tiers, ranging from basic solids to threaded mating pairs and functional gear trains. Each system receives a task specification and must produce a complete CAD model. Submissions are evaluated with task-specific checks for dimensions, placement, mating geometry and thread profiles, including Blender-based rigid-body simulation of assembly motion.

Initial results show that CAD-bench is not saturated by the evaluated systems. The strongest standalone model reaches 59.9% overall, while the strongest agent configuration reaches 67.7%. Both standalone models and agents still fail frequently on interfaces, standards-like details and mechanisms. CAD-bench exposes a gap between producing runnable CAD artifacts and producing designs that satisfy mechanically meaningful requirements.

Results

The evaluation includes 19 standalone-model configurations and 32 agent configurations, each tested on all 17 tasks. On the four functional tasks, their mean scores are 3.7% and 14.1%, respectively.

Scoring

The overall score is the weighted average of the average scores for the Easy, Medium, Hard and Extreme levels, with respective weights 1, 2, 3 and 4. The Extreme level consists of the four functional tasks. Scores measure average credit, not the percentage of fully correct designs.
